User guide

This page covers the solver features beyond the quickstart: FMM acceleration, volume sources, many-right-hand-side solves, and visualization. For large distributed four-index runs, see Lattice campaigns.

FMM-accelerated solves

For larger problems, replace the dense operator with an FMM-accelerated LinearMap and solve with GMRES. In 3D, lhs_dielectric_box3d_fmm3d_corrected adds the near-field correction needed for accuracy near the surface.

using DielectricBIE

interface = single_dielectric_box2d(1.0, 1.0, 8, 0.2, 0.05, 5.0, 1.0, Float64)
lhs_fmm = lhs_dielectric_box2d_fmm2d(interface, 1e-12)
rhs = rhs_dielectric_box2d(interface, PointSource((0.1, 0.1), 1.0), 5.0)

# solve_gmres(A, b, atol, rtol)
sigma = solve_gmres(lhs_fmm, rhs, 1e-12, 1e-12)

Boxes with GCQ edge panels

At the edges of a dielectric box the surface density is singular, σ ~ Σₖ cₖ s^{pₖ} in the distance s to the edge. The powers pₖ come from the 2D wedge determinant (edge_density_powers, junction_powers), and edge_gcq_rule builds a weighted generalized Chebyshev quadrature (GCQ) on [0,1] that integrates the expansion times smooth functions. single_dielectric_box3d_gcq uses it on TensorPanels: GCQ across each edge × Gauss–Legendre along it, GCQ × GCQ at the vertices, and dyadic refinement toward the vertices only. Near-singular interactions are a sparse correction added to the FMM operator.

using DielectricBIE, LinearAlgebra
import DielectricBIE as BI

eps_in = 10.0
rule = edge_gcq_rule(eps_in, 1.0; tol = 1e-12)          # 28 nodes
interface = single_dielectric_box3d_gcq(1.0, 1.0, 1.0, eps_in, 1.0;
                                        nlev = 3, n_quad = 8, rule)
A, stats = lhs_dielectric_box3d_gcq_fmm3d(interface, 1e-12)
rhs = rhs_dielectric_box3d(interface, PointSource((0.1, 0.15, 0.2), 1.0), eps_in)
sigma, _ = solve_dielectric_box3d_gcq(A, rhs, interface; rtol = 1e-12)
dot(BI.all_weights(interface), sigma) - (1 - 1 / eps_in)   # ≈ 5e-9

The error then falls by about 3× per vertex level, the rate of the vertex singularity (σ ~ ρ^{−0.397} for ε = 10). Use a Gauss–Legendre order of at least 6: lower orders cannot resolve the part of σ driven by the source.

The rule integrates the edge expansion up to a largest exponent qmax. The density alone is resolved with qmax ≈ 10, but the far-field rows integrate the density times the kernel, which adds smooth content: for targets one to two panel widths away the error is about 1e-9 at qmax = 10 and reaches 1e-12 at qmax ≈ 20–24, for 4–5 more nodes per edge panel.

Several bodies, high contrast and perfect conductors

Several boxes are meshed separately (one single_dielectric_box3d_gcq per body, placed with center) and solved together. solve_dielectric_boxes_gcq joins the bodies, assembles the FMM operator and the right-hand side (each point charge scaled by the permittivity of the region containing it), and solves with one total-charge constraint per body:

using DielectricBIE, LinearAlgebra

R = 1e4                                                   # contrast; Inf = perfect conductor
rule = edge_gcq_rule(R, 1.0; tol = 1e-12, qmax = 24.0)
parts = [single_dielectric_box3d_gcq(1.0, 1.0, 1.0, R, 1.0; nlev = 4, n_quad = 8, rule, center = c)
         for c in ((-0.75, 0.0, 0.0), (0.75, 0.0, 0.0))]
sol = solve_dielectric_boxes_gcq(parts, PointSource((0.0, 0.9, 0.1), 1.0))
sol.σ, sol.interface, sol.bodies                          # density, joined interface, node ranges

The total-charge constraint. At high contrast (and for eps_in = Inf) the plain system does not pin down each body's total charge: its error grows like εin/εout. Gauss's law fixes the total charge of every body,

Q_c = Σ_{charges q inside c} q (1/ε_out − 1/ε_in(c))      (0 for a body without charge),

and the solver imposes it through a rank-one term per body, keeping the system square:

(A − ½ Σ_c u_c w_cᵀ) σ = b − ½ Σ_c u_c Q_c,    u_c = 1_c / |∂c|,

with wc the quadrature weights of body c. deflate = false gives the plain system; `boxchargeconstraintsbuilds the constraints forsolvedielectricbox3dgcq` when the operator is assembled by hand.

Perfect conductors. Pass eps_in = Inf: dielectric_tau gives τ = −1/2, and edge_density_powers(Inf, 1.0) gives the conductor edge powers γ_k = kπ/(2π − α) (for a right angle p = −1/3, 1/3, 1, 5/3, …, the limit of the dielectric roots). A source inside a conductor is rejected.

Touching boxes. Where two boxes share an edge, that edge is a junction of four sectors and needs its own rule, e.g. for two boxes of permittivity R meeting along the z-axis edge with vacuum in the other two quadrants (p₁ = (4/π) atan(1/√R) − 1 → −1 as R grows):

r_shared = edge_gcq_rule(junction_powers(fill(pi / 2, 4), [R, 1.0, R, 1.0]; gmax = 2.5) .- 1;
                         tol = 1e-12, qmax = 24.0)
shared(m, axis) = (axis == 3 && abs(m[1]) < 1e-12 && abs(m[2]) < 1e-12) ? r_shared : nothing
parts = [single_dielectric_box3d_gcq(1.0, 1.0, 1.0, R, 1.0; nlev = 4, n_quad = 8, rule,
                                     edge_rule = shared, center = c)
         for c in ((0.5, 0.5, 0.5), (-0.5, -0.5, 0.5))]

The shared edge itself adds no local error at any contrast; the remaining limit is the vertex at its end, whose exponent also tends to 0 as R grows. Touching perfect conductors (p₁ = −1) are not supported: the density is no longer integrable.

Boxes sharing faces. Meshing boxes separately is exact only while they touch along edges or at vertices. A face shared by two boxes must be meshed once, as an εA | εB interface, not as two coincident box-to-exterior surfaces. multi_dielectric_box3d_gcq takes the same box list as multi_dielectric_box3d:

  • It puts the boxes on the grid of all their face coordinates and meshes every interface rectangle once.
  • It gives every edge the GCQ rule of its own junction, from junction_powers. These are the cube edges, the triple junctions (vacuum π, εA π/2, εB π/2) where a shared face meets the outside, and four-sector edges; equal junctions share one rule.
  • It refines toward true vertices only.
boxes = [(center = (-0.5, 0.0, 0.0), Lx = 1.0, Ly = 1.0, Lz = 1.0),
         (center = (0.5, 0.0, 0.0), Lx = 1.0, Ly = 1.0, Lz = 1.0)]
mesh = multi_dielectric_box3d_gcq(boxes, [2.0, 10.0], 1.0; nlev = 4, n_quad = 8,
                                  tol = 1e-12, qmax = 24.0)
sol = solve_dielectric_boxes_gcq(mesh, PointSource((-0.4, 0.1, 0.15), 1.0))

The total-charge constraints are imposed per cluster of boxes joined by shared faces (mesh.clusters, node ranges mesh.bodies), the charge inside a closed surface around the cluster. A surface around one box of a face-sharing pair would cut through its neighbour. At high contrast the constraint is less effective for such a cluster than for a single box. The near-null mode's left vector is weighted by the permittivity jumps across the shared face, not constant over the cluster. A cluster can also have further near-null modes: two high-contrast boxes joined through a low-contrast one carry a +q/−q mode, which the cluster's total charge does not fix. Treat high-contrast face-sharing clusters with care.

Volume sources and precomputed fields

A VolumeSource is a volume charge density sampled on a quadrature grid: positions (3 × N), quadrature weights, and density values. Its free-space Laplace potential can drive the right-hand side of a dielectric problem on an interface that is adaptively refined to resolve that source:

using DielectricBIE

# A Gaussian charge sampled on a uniform midpoint grid: positions (3×N), weights, density.
n, h = 24, 1.2 / 24
xs = range(-0.6 + h / 2, 0.6 - h / 2; length = n)
positions = reduce(hcat, [[x, y, z] for x in xs for y in xs for z in xs])
density = [exp(-sum(abs2, p) / (2 * 0.08^2)) for p in eachcol(positions)]
vs = VolumeSource(positions, fill(h^3, n^3), density)

# single_dielectric_box3d_rhs_adaptive(Lx, Ly, Lz, n_quad, source,
#                                       eps_src, l_ec, rhs_atol, eps_in, eps_out[, T])
interface = single_dielectric_box3d_rhs_adaptive(
    1.0, 1.0, 1.0, 4, vs, 1.0, 0.25, 1e-3, 4.0, 1.0, Float64)

rhs = rhs_dielectric_box3d_hybrid(interface, vs, 1.0, 1e-6)

When the same source is reused for many target batches or assemblies, build the field once and reuse it. PrecomputedVolumeField stores a truncated spectral representation; in-box targets are evaluated with a type-2 NUFFT and out-of-box targets with the FMM:

field = PrecomputedVolumeField(vs; tol = 1e-6)

phi  = volume_field_potential(field, targets)   # targets: 3×n  ->  length-n potential
grad = volume_field_gradient(field, targets)    #              ->  3×n gradient
rhs  = rhs_dielectric_box3d_field(interface, field, 1.0)

Many right-hand sides and four-index integrals

For several sources sharing one interface, assemble and block-solve all right-hand sides at once, then contract into the four-index Coulomb matrix V[a, b]:

using DielectricBIE

# vss::Vector{VolumeSource} on a shared interface that resolves all of them
# (e.g. built with multi_dielectric_box3d_rhs_adaptive)
sigma, stats = solve_dielectric_box3d_block(interface, vss; rtol = 1e-6)
V = four_index_matrix(interface, vss, sigma; lhs_tol = 1e-6, volume_tol = 1e-6)

Operator backends: SCTL.jl and self-defined quadratures

The 3D operator τ I + Dᵀ is assembled from two interchangeable parts: dielectric_operator(interface; far, near).

  • far, a FarField, evaluates the adjoint double layer with the smooth node rule (the interface's quadrature points and weights):
    • FMM3DFarField(tol) is FMM3D, the default;
    • SCTLFarField(tol = …) is SCTL.jl's FMM. It uses PVFMM with OpenMP and no MPI, and keeps its octree across GMRES iterations.
  • near, a NearQuadrature, returns the sparse correction C (accurate minus node rule) for nearby panel pairs:
    • UpsampledNearQuad is the default for FlatPanel interfaces;
    • GCQNearQuad is the default for GCQ TensorPanel interfaces;
    • SCTLNearQuad is SCTL's singular quadrature, for FlatPanel interfaces with n_quad in 4, 8, 12, 16 or 20, combined with SCTLFarField;
    • your own quadrature works too, as described below.

The result, a DielectricOperator, supports A * x and A * X for an N × K block, so it serves both Krylov.gmres and Krylov.block_gmres.

SCTL.jl is a separate, unregistered package with a C++ part. Install it once, then load it next to DielectricBIE:

using Pkg
Pkg.develop(url = "https://github.com/xuanzhaogao/SCTL.jl")   # Pkg.add(url) fails on its git submodules
Pkg.build("SCTL")                                             # compiles the C++ shim (a few minutes)
using DielectricBIE, SCTL, Krylov

interface = single_dielectric_box3d(1.2, 0.8, 0.6, 8, 0.05, 4.0, 1.0, Float64)
rhs = rhs_dielectric_box3d(interface, PointSource((0.1, 0.1, 0.1), 1.0), 4.0)

A = dielectric_operator(interface; far = SCTLFarField(tol = 1e-10), near = SCTLNearQuad())
sigma, stats = Krylov.gmres(A, rhs; rtol = 1e-8)

Things to know about the SCTL backends:

  • Accuracy. With 40,000 or more nodes SCTL's far field is PVFMM, whose gradients lose about 1.5 digits. So SCTLFarField(tol = t) runs PVFMM with two extra digits, and SCTLNearQuad builds SCTL's operator at accuracy t / 100. PVFMM reaches about 1e-11 at best. Below 40,000 nodes the far field is an exact direct sum.
  • Precomputation. The first use for a given number of digits precomputes PVFMM's translation operators and caches them (see SCTL.fmm_cache_dir()). This takes seconds for these Laplace kernels. Before starting many parallel jobs, run one small solve first.
  • Threads. SCTL uses OpenMP (OMP_NUM_THREADS). While SCTL runs, Julia's BLAS is limited to one thread. Krylov's vector operations are also faster with BLAS.set_num_threads(1) on many cores. On 96 cores the SCTL solve of the 288,256-node box took 0.6 s that way, against 7.3 s with 96 BLAS threads.

Batch mode

solve_dielectric_box3d_block, solve_dielectric_lattice_batch and the campaign solve phase take far / near too. Block GMRES then applies the chosen operator to all K right-hand sides of an iteration:

sigma, stats = solve_dielectric_box3d_block(interface, vss; rtol = 1e-8,
                                            far = SCTLFarField(tol = 1e-10), near = SCTLNearQuad())

FMM3D evaluates the K densities in one vectorized FMM. SCTL's FMM evaluates them one after another on the same octree. For a lattice campaign, add a [backend] table to the .toml (far = "sctl", optionally near = "sctl"; see campaign_backend). run_phase then loads SCTL.jl on every worker.

Self-defined near quadratures (e.g. for GCQ panels)

A near quadrature only has to produce the sparse correction. Any of these work as near:

  • a sparse matrix C;
  • a function interface -> C;
  • a subtype of NearQuadrature with a near_corrections method.

The rows of C are targets and the columns are sources, both in the node order of the interface. C[i, j] is the accurate value of Dᵀ[i, j] minus the node-rule value w_j n_i·(x_i − y_j) / (4π|x_i − y_j|³). Pairs that are not near get no entry.

struct MyGCQ <: DielectricBIE.NearQuadrature
    quad_tol::Float64
end
DielectricBIE.near_corrections(q::MyGCQ, interface) =
    first(laplace3d_DT_tensor_corrections(interface; quad_tol = q.quad_tol))   # or your own rule

interface = single_dielectric_box3d_gcq(1.0, 1.0, 1.0, 10.0, 1.0; nlev = 2, n_quad = 6)
A = dielectric_operator(interface; far = SCTLFarField(tol = 1e-12), near = MyGCQ(1e-12))

Building a near quadrature on SCTL's pieces

SCTL.jl exposes the steps of its own near correction as Julia data (see its manual page "Near-field pieces"). In DielectricBIE they are available as follows:

  • SCTLNearCorrection(tol = …) is SCTL's complete correction as a sparse C, usable with either far field.
  • sctl_near_list(interface; tol, radius) is SCTL's classification alone: which interface nodes are near which panel. It works for any panel type when you pass the radii.
  • dt_node_rule_block(interface, k, targets) is the node-rule block that a correction subtracts.

So a quadrature written in Julia only has to produce the accurate rows. This example uses SCTL's classification with DielectricBIE's adaptive quadtree for the rows. The tree's moments use the panel's Lagrange basis, so they act on nodal values. Targets in the panel's plane keep the node rule, since Dᵀ vanishes there:

using DielectricBIE, SCTL
import DielectricBIE as BI
using LinearAlgebra

interface = single_dielectric_box3d(1.0, 1.0, 1.0, 4, 0.5, 4.0, 1.0, Float64)
nl = sctl_near_list(interface; tol = 1e-10)              # SCTL's near test
X, Nrm = BI._interface_targets_normals(interface)
owner = reduce(vcat, [fill(k, BI.num_points(p)) for (k, p) in enumerate(interface.panels)])
cfg = BI.AdaptiveConfig(atol = 1e-12)

coplanar(p, q) = abs(dot(p.normal, q.normal)) > 1 - 1e-12 && abs(dot(p.normal, q.points[1] .- p.points[1])) < 1e-12

accurate = map(eachindex(interface.panels)) do k                   # your quadrature goes here
    p = interface.panels[k]
    ts = SCTL.near_targets(nl, k)
    B = dt_node_rule_block(interface, k, ts)               # start from the node rule
    for k2 in unique(owner[ts])
        coplanar(p, interface.panels[k2]) && continue      # Dᵀ = 0 in the panel's plane
        rows = findall(t -> owner[t] == k2, ts)
        K = zeros(length(rows), BI.num_points(p))
        BI.adaptive_block_moments_tree!(K, p, [Tuple(X[:, t]) for t in ts[rows]], interface.panels[k2].normal, :DT, cfg)
        B[rows, :] = K
    end
    B
end
plain = [dt_node_rule_block(interface, k, SCTL.near_targets(nl, k)) for k in eachindex(interface.panels)]
C = SCTL.assemble_near_correction(nl, accurate, [BI.num_points(p) for p in interface.panels]; subtract = plain)

A = dielectric_operator(interface; far = SCTLFarField(tol = 1e-12), near = C)

On this box, A agrees with near = SCTLNearCorrection(tol = 1e-12) to 1e-9. That comparison is part of the tests and is a quick check of a new rule. For panels that are not FlatPanels, give the radii: sctl_near_list(interface; radius = r), with one radius per node or one for all.

SCTL's quadrature on your own panels

SCTLProxyNearQuad applies SCTL's near quadrature to panels that are not SCTL elements, as long as their density is a polynomial through the nodes. For each such panel it does the following:

  1. Builds a proxy QuadElemList element with the panel's geometry.
  2. Uses SCTL's classification and near-singular rule on the proxy.
  3. Maps the rows back to the panel's nodes through the interpolation from the panel's nodes to the proxy's. That interpolation is exact for a polynomial density.

The other panels get fallback, which only computes their columns. Two keywords choose how panels are split:

  • polynomial decides which panels SCTL handles. The default is default_polynomial_panel: every FlatPanel, and TensorPanels with kind == :gl.
  • fallback handles the rest; it defaults to GCQNearQuad().
interface = single_dielectric_box3d_gcq(1.0, 1.0, 1.0, 10.0, 1.0; nlev = 2, n_quad = 8)
A = dielectric_operator(interface; far = SCTLFarField(tol = 1e-12),
                        near = SCTLProxyNearQuad(tol = 1e-12, fallback = GCQNearQuad(quad_tol = 1e-12)))

On this box, which has 168 panels and 35,328 nodes, it agrees with the all-GCQ operator to 2e-13. It is not faster, though. GCQ's setup, about 20 s on 8 threads, goes almost entirely to the 120 edge and corner panels, whose singular densities SCTL cannot integrate. GCQ's rows for the 48 Gauss–Legendre panels take only 0.3 s, against 1.3 s for SCTL's.

Use SCTLProxyNearQuad for panel types without a near rule of their own: curved or parametrized patches, or new panel kinds. Any type works once it has a parametrization, DielectricBIESCTLExt._panel_param. It also serves as an independent check of a new rule.

A quadrature that can compute only some panels' columns can implement near_corrections(q, interface, sources), as GCQNearQuad does. Otherwise the default computes all columns and drops the others.

The GCQ solvers take the same keywords: solve_dielectric_boxes_gcq(parts, sources; far = SCTLFarField(tol = 1e-12), near = MyGCQ(1e-12)).

Potentials off the surface

Potentials at points off the interface are needed for evaluate_batch_potential, four_index_matrix and a campaign's eval phase. They are computed by a PotentialEvaluator through potential_operator(ev, interface, targets):

  • HCubaturePotential(tol), the default, is laplace3d_pottrg_fmm3d_corrected_hcubature:

    1. panels near the targets are refined;
    2. FMM3D evaluates the node rule for every density column;
    3. targets within 5 × (panel size) / n_quad of a panel's center get an HCubature correction.

  • SCTLPotential(tol = …) is SCTL's BoundaryIntegralOp for the single layer with the targets set:

    1. a near test per node (the Bernstein radius at tol);
    2. for each near target, the panel split at the target's closest point and refined dyadically toward it;
    3. PVFMM for the far field, with the octree built once for all densities.

    It needs FlatPanels with n_quad in 4, 8, 12, 16 or 20, and using SCTL.

Φ = evaluate_batch_potential(interface, Σ, sources, targets; lhs_tol = 1e-8, volume_tol = 1e-8,
                             potential = SCTLPotential(tol = 1e-8))
V = four_index_matrix(interface, sources, Σ; lhs_tol = 1e-8, volume_tol = 1e-8, potential = SCTLPotential(tol = 1e-8))

In a campaign, potential = "sctl" in the [backend] table selects it for the eval phase.

Here is a comparison on a box of 80,896 nodes (n_quad = 8, l_ec = 0.05), with 1.1 million targets: a cloud around the box, plus 100,000 points between 1e-4 and 1e-1 from the surface. The run used 32 threads at tolerance 1e-8. Errors are against a tight adaptive cubature over every panel.

evaluatorsetupper densitymax error (cloud)max error (closest 20 targets)
HCubaturePotential10.1 s6.5 s7e-108e-4
SCTLPotential1.0 s0.14 s8e-122e-12

HCubaturePotential's correction radius is measured from panel centers and doesn't depend on the tolerance. That causes two kinds of error:

  • Targets close to the surface near panel edges or corners, of their own panel or of its neighbours, get no correction, hence the 8e-4 above.
  • Interfaces with large panels relative to n_quad can be wrong even far from the surface: 5e-4 at the grid points of the coarse 24-panel box in the tests.

Visualization

Plotting helpers (viz_2d, viz_3d, viz_3d_surface, viz_3d_interface_solution, viz_3d_zslice, plot_campaign_geometry) live in a Makie package extension. Install Makie and a backend, then load the backend alongside DielectricBIE:

using Pkg
Pkg.add(["Makie", "CairoMakie"])
using CairoMakie
using DielectricBIE

interface = single_dielectric_box3d(1.2, 0.8, 0.6, 4, 0.2, 4.0, 1.0, Float64)
fig = viz_3d(interface)
